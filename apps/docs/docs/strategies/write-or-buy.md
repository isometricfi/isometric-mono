# Write or Buy

Two paths through the same market. Both sides are taking a position on where BTC will be at expiry.

## Write — get paid for taking on risk

Writing pays you upfront because you're agreeing to a payoff if BTC runs past your strike. You're effectively betting BTC will stay below your strike for the term. If you're right, you keep the premium and your collateral.

The market sets the premium based on how likely the strike is to be hit. A +5% strike over 1 day is unlikely to get there, so the premium is small. A +5% strike over 14 days has more time to move, so writers demand a larger premium for that risk. Same strike, different risk profile, different price.

## Buy — pay for an asymmetric bet

Buying pays a known amount upfront in exchange for unbounded upside if BTC clears the strike. You're betting BTC moves through your strike before the term ends. If you're right, your payout scales with how far it moves. If not, you lose only what you put in.

Cheap premiums look attractive, but they reflect the market's view that the strike is unlikely to be hit. Expensive premiums tend to be on shorter terms with closer strikes — situations where reaching the strike is more plausible. The leverage you get from a cheap option is high precisely because the move is unlikely.

## Both sides are pricing the same thing

Both buyer and writer are taking a view on the probability that BTC ends above the strike at expiry. The premium is the price the market settles on for that probability:

- Longer term, more time for BTC to move → higher premium.
- Closer strike, easier to reach → higher premium.
- Higher recent volatility → higher premium across the board.

When you write, you're taking the other side of someone's bet. When you buy, you're paying someone to take the other side of yours. Most active traders do both, depending on their view at the time.
